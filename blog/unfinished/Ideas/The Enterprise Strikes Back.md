**The Time is Now: Consumers Need Enterprise-Level Data Access**

Hey everyone, Zach here. I’ve been a tech enthusiast and tinkerer for as long as I can remember. But lately, I've been bumping my head against a wall, a digital wall built by the very companies I rely on daily. We're not talking about complex server management here; I'm talking about basic control over my own data across the various cloud services and online tools we all use.

It's 2024, and the average consumer isn't just passively clicking "Next" anymore. We're more tech-savvy than ever, building smart homes, curating digital libraries, and managing entire families' digital lives. We're not all writing code, but we understand how data moves and we want more control. The sad truth, however, is the tools we are provided to achieve this goal have simply not kept up with the pace at which user class consumer intelligence has improved. We’re left stuck in a world of disparate apps and web interfaces, trying to wrangle data silos like we’re herding cats.

Take, for example, my struggles with Google Photos and Amazon Music. I have photos scattered across multiple accounts, backup drives, and devices and Amazon Music playlists and libraries for me, my wife, and our kids and every change and filter has to be managed via the phone or web apps. Why can’t I, the owner of the data, have programmatic access to these services? I am not an outlier. There are [over 1 Billion Google Photos users](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Ftechcrunch.com%2F2023%2F03%2F01%2Fgoogle-photos-has-1-billion-plus-users%2F) and [over 55 Million Amazon Music users](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.statista.com%2Fstatistics%2F1269224%2Famazon-music-subscribers-worldwide%2F). These are not niche products. These are tools being used by a substantial portion of the planet!

My frustrations extend beyond media management. As a parent, the lack of granular control over family devices is maddening. Google Family Link, Microsoft Family, even the Meta Oculus Quest parental controls – they’re all essentially walled gardens, forcing me to manage each device through the specific platform’s app, leaving little to no space for custom automation or integrations with my other tools. These platforms have [over 50 million downloads on Android alone](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fplay.google.com%2Fstore%2Fapps%2Fdetails%3Fid%3Dcom.google.android.apps.kids.familylink%26hl%3Den_US%26gl%3DUS), highlighting just how many people are in a similar situation. Why are there no robust, consumer-facing APIs for these services? It's not about needing to be a developer to manage my family’s devices; it's about having the option to do so.

The major tech players aren't blind to the power of APIs. They understand that having strong, programmable interfaces for their enterprise products is good for business, and it's time that same logic was applied to consumer data. If a company expects me to use their platform to manage my family's information they ought to provide me the same level of access to my own information as an enterprise customer would expect for business critical data.

Here’s a few ways these tech giants could better support their users:

- **Google:** Expose a more comprehensive API for Google Family Link, including the ability to manage screen time, app usage, and location tracking via RESTful calls. Allow consumers to easily export a full history of activity data. Similarly, an open API for Google Home would allow for deeper integrations with home automation tools, going far beyond what’s offered today.

- **Microsoft:** The same applies to Microsoft Family. Consumers should have the ability to programmatically manage their family’s settings and devices in a more intuitive way. They have done a wonderful job of providing robust APIs in products such as Microsoft 365, and now it's time to bring that over to family services.

- **Meta:** The Oculus Quest parental controls are incredibly limited for consumers. Opening a set of APIs around user time spent, games played, and applications launched would empower consumers to create custom reporting and automation. Simply opening up an endpoint I could use to lock/unlock headsets would go miles as well.

It’s time for companies to recognize that their users aren’t just passive consumers; they are active participants in their own digital lives. We’ve grown past the need for simplistic “click-here” interfaces, and we crave more control, more customization, and more agency over our own data. Companies that embrace this shift will not only earn greater customer loyalty, but will also unlock possibilities we cannot even imagine yet.
